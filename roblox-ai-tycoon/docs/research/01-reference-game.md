## 2. Core loop (verified pieces, step by step)
Official description (via search summaries) says: you start in a bedroom with a laptop, train AI models, buy GPUs, hire engineers/researchers/marketers, move through bigger offices, take on big contracts, handle crises, beat rival AI labs, build your own AI chip, then sell the company for billions and start again. [V]

Step-by-step as best reconstructed:
1. Spawn in a bedroom; tutorial uses the laptop. Settings (gear icon on laptop) holds the code box. [V]
2. Configure a model: size, data and personality (from the game's own blurb). [V-weak]
3. Train it (time-based; faster with more compute). [V/I]
4. Launch it. A good model brings thousands of users, a great one goes viral. [V]
5. Users produce Cash; income continues while offline. [V]
6. Cash buys GPUs/servers (racks fill the space), better machines, staff and bigger buildings. A fan GPU tier list is advertised. [V]
7. Hardware gating: bigger models require hardware you must upgrade to meet. [V, Destructoid]
8. Side systems named in the blurb: contracts (big paying jobs), crises (negative events), rival AI labs, an own-chip project, robots/nanobots. [V, thinly described]
9. Endgame: sell the company for billions and restart with a bonus (rebirth/prestige). [V]

## 9. Opportunities (25) for our original game
Each pairs a gap in the reference with an improvement.
1. Real compute economics: buy vs rent cloud, power draw, cooling, depreciation, hardware generations, supply shortages. (Reference: buy GPUs, numbers go up.)
2. Data as a first-class resource: licensing, scraping legal risk, synthetic data, cleaning, quality vs quantity, data moats.
3. Meaningful model design: architecture, size, data mix, training length, with loss curves and diminishing returns rather than a size slider plus "personality".
4. Training can fail: divergence, overfitting, bugs, hallucination, bias; evaluate before ship. Creates tension and skill.
5. Benchmarks and public leaderboards: contamination, benchmark gaming, reputation effects.
6. Active rivals: NPC (and later player) labs release models, poach staff, undercut price, open-source your lead away. The reference only mentions "beat rivals".
7. Talent: researchers with specialties, morale, burnout, salary, poaching, culture. Reference only "hire staff".
8. Funding realism: seed to Series A+, dilution, valuation, burn rate, runway, IPO/acquisition. Replace "sell for billions" with branching exits.
9. Product layer: API, consumer app, enterprise contracts; inference cost per query so scale can lose money; pricing choices.
10. Launch events with texture: press, social feed, reviews, outages from demand spikes, viral moment, backlash, shown as a live in-game feed.
11. Safety, ethics, regulation: red-teaming, incidents, audits, region-specific laws; speed vs safety trade-off with real consequences.
12. Prestige that is not a pure reset: legacy research tree, spin-off companies, endowed labs, "second company" with different strategy.
13. Multiplayer: shared market, co-op labs, compute trading, poaching between players, seasonal leaderboards. Reference seems solo.
14. Player agency in 3D: build and lay out your own office and datacenter (aisles, power, cooling), see staff at desks, not just auto-upgraded tiers.
15. Events and crises with causes: chip export bans, outages, data breach, weight leak, talent raid, hype/bubble cycles that shift the economy; counterplay available.
16. Active minigames: debug a run, interview candidates, demo-day pitch, incident response. Reduce pure idle waiting.
17. Eras and content cadence: hardware generations and paradigm shifts as seasons; public roadmap, wiki and Discord from day one.
18. Fair monetization: cosmetics (office themes, avatars, decorations), time-savers that never gate core progress; avoid like-gated code spam as the main hook.
19. Education layer: tooltips and a glossary explaining parameters, tokens, FLOPs, inference; appeals to parents and teachers and sets us apart.
20. Clear goals system: always show the next goal, quests, daily/weekly objectives; reference seems purchase-driven only.
21. Transparent economy: show formulas, ROI per upgrade, break-even times so players can optimize; reference hides numbers (fan tier lists fill the gap).
22. Custom chip as a real branch: design-vs-foundry trade-offs, tape-out risk, yield, partnerships (the reference names it but depth is unknown).
23. Contracts with substance: clients with requirements, SLAs, penalties, reputation; negotiating, not just clicking.
24. Customer and user simulation: segments (consumer, developer, enterprise), churn, support load, trust metrics.
25. Accessibility and polish: low-end device performance (common tycoon pain: physics lag, per DevForum threads), mobile-first UI, clear tutorial (Game Dev Tycoon's weak point).
